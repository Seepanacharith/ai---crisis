# Earthquake Safety, Response & Survival Protocol

## Overview
Earthquakes occur without warning due to sudden slippage along fault lines. Immediate protective action during shaking is critical to prevent injury or loss of life.

## During Shaking (The DROP, COVER, AND HOLD ON Standard)
1. DROP onto your hands and knees. This position prevents you from being knocked over and allows you to stay low.
2. COVER your head and neck with your arms. If a sturdy table or desk is nearby, crawl underneath it for shelter.
3. HOLD ON to your shelter until shaking stops. Be prepared to move with your shelter if it shifts.
4. If indoors: Stay inside! Do NOT run outside during shaking. Avoid doorways, glass windows, mirrors, lighting fixtures, and heavy furniture that could fall.
5. If in bed: Stay in bed, turn face down, and cover your head with a pillow.
6. If outdoors: Move away from buildings, streetlights, overhead utility wires, trees, and overpasses. Drop to the ground and cover your head.
7. If driving: Pull over safely to the side of the road away from overpasses, bridges, power lines, and trees. Stay inside the vehicle until shaking stops.

## Immediately After Shaking
1. Expect aftershocks. Secondary earthquakes can cause further structural damage.
2. Check yourself and others for injuries. Administer first aid if trained. Do not move seriously injured persons unless they are in immediate danger.
3. Check for gas leaks, electrical wiring damage, and water pipe breaks. If you smell gas or suspect a leak, turn off the main gas valve, open windows, and leave the building immediately.
4. Do NOT use matches, lighters, open flames, or electrical switches if gas leakage is suspected.
5. Use flashlights instead of candles.
6. Wear sturdy shoes to protect your feet from broken glass and debris.

## Structural Hazards & Evacuation
1. Inspect walls, chimneys, and roof structures for deep cracks or tilt before staying inside.
2. If forced to evacuate, take your emergency Go-Bag, turn off main utility valves, and walk via designated clear stairwells. Do NOT use elevators.
3. Establish communication with family members via SMS or emergency messaging platforms rather than voice calls to avoid network congestion.
