# Flood Emergency Safety & Preparedness Guidelines

## Overview
Floods are one of the most common and dangerous natural disasters. Flash floods can occur within minutes of intense rainfall, dam failures, or storm surges.

## Before a Flood (Preparation & Mitigation)
1. Know your area's flood risk and evacuation routes.
2. Build an Emergency Preparedness Kit including at least 3 days of non-perishable food, water (1 gallon per person per day), flashlight, first-aid supplies, and portable power banks.
3. Elevate critical home electrical appliances, water heaters, and electrical panels above potential flood levels.
4. Keep important personal documents (passports, property papers, insurance, identification) in waterproof bags or digital cloud storage.
5. Install check valves in sewer traps to prevent floodwaters from backing up into drains.

## During a Flood (Immediate Response)
1. Listen to official weather broadcasts, local authority instructions, and emergency alerts.
2. Move immediately to higher ground or upper levels of sturdy buildings. Do NOT stay in basements or low-lying structures.
3. Turn off main electricity switches, gas valves, and water supplies if instructed to do so or before evacuating.
4. NEVER walk, swim, or drive through flowing floodwaters. Just 6 inches of moving water can knock an adult down, and 12 inches can sweep away a car.
5. Avoid contact with floodwater as it may be contaminated with sewage, chemicals, or live electrical wires.
6. If trapped in a building, move to the highest floor or roof. Signal for help using a bright cloth, flashlight, or whistle. Do not climb into enclosed attics without roof access.

## After a Flood (Recovery & Health)
1. Return home only after local authorities declare the area safe.
2. Inspect homes for structural damage before entering. Watch for snakes, insects, or animals that may have entered during the flood.
3. Do not drink tap water or use water from flooded wells until certified safe by health authorities. Use boiled or bottled water only.
4. Discard all food that has come into contact with floodwater.
5. Take photographs of flood damage for insurance claims before beginning cleanup. Wear heavy gloves, boots, and N95 masks during cleanup to avoid toxic mold and contaminants.
